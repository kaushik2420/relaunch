# Empathy principles

Our users are coming off a hard moment. Most other tools either guilt them
("you haven't applied today!") or oversell ("crush your job search!").
We won't.

These are the rules. Don't ship copy that breaks them without a discussion.

## 1. Forward-looking stats only

| ❌ Don't say | ✅ Say |
|------------|--------|
| "Rejected from 4 of 9 applications" | "5 active applications, 3 awaiting reply" |
| "Below-average response rate" | "Top 30% candidate for 64% of roles we found" |
| "No applications today" | "Today is a good day to rest" |

## 2. Streaks without shame

Show "Day 12 of your search" in soft black. Never red. Never orange.

If a user pauses and comes back, open with: **"Welcome back — no judgment, here are 5 fresh ones."**

## 3. One-tap relief

Every notification surface needs an "I need a break" — 3/7/14 days.
The empathy-banner copy when paused: **"We're here when you're ready. Your sheet is safe."**

## 4. Money up front

Show expected CTC on every job card. Tip-toeing about salary is patronizing
to people thinking about runway. Don't hide it under a tooltip.

## 5. "Why" before "do this"

When we surface a referrer, always lead with *why this person can help*
(shared previous company, same college). Never just "connect with them."
Reasons give the user agency.

## 6. No urgency theatre

Don't use red badges, countdowns, or "act now" framing. Our product is daily,
not real-time. Calm is a feature.

## 7. Decline gracefully

If we don't have matches today, say so honestly:
> "Quieter day on our side — only 2 strong matches. Tomorrow's batch already looks bigger."

Not:
> "No matches found."

## 8. The hardship line

Every paywall, every trial-end email, must include:

> If money is tight right now, write to us at hello@relaunch.app — we have a hardship program for folks still in the search.

This is not negotiable.

## 9. Things we don't do

- Push notifications outside of the user's chosen send time
- Reflective listening that amplifies negative emotion
- Asking the user to "fix" themselves before we can help
- Stack-ranking users against each other
- "X people applied to this role in the last hour"

## 10. The voice test

If you can imagine a kind friend who's been laid off saying it to another
friend who's just been laid off, it's right. If it sounds like LinkedIn,
it's wrong.
